A survey of Diptera species causing cutaneous myiases on sheep in Botucatu, S~o Paulo, Brazil was made to determine seasonal incidence, predilection sites and the factors predisposing to infestation. Sheep were checked daily for myiases for one year. At two week intervals larvae from wounds were collected for identification. Only larvae of Dermatobia hominis and Cochliomyia hominivorax were found. Myiases due to C. hominivorax were observed during the whole year with high incidence from January to April. The feet, vulva, tail and scrotum were most frequently infested. Wounds were the commonest predisposing factor.

### INTRODUCTION
In the neotropical region the main species that cause primary myiasis of sheep are Cochliomyia hominivorax, Dermatobia hominis and Oestrus ovis. Other species of Calliphoridae, Fannidae and Sarcophagidae are cited by Guimar~es et al. (1983) as facultati,~e parasites.

In 1978 Guimar~es et al. reported for the first time the presence of three species of Chrysomyia in Brazil and concluded that they had been introduced.

The many Diptera species that potentially cause myiasis, especially of sheep (James and Harwood, 1969) and the damage caused by them, emphasises the importance of aetiological studies in each region and the factors that predispose animals to infestation.

This work was conducted to survey the Diptera species associated with sheep cutaneous myiasis in the municipality of Botucatu, their seasonal incidence, the body region most frequently affected and the predisposing factors to infestation.

### MATERIALS AND METHODS
The observations were made at the sheep breeding farm of the College o f Veterinary Medicine and Animal Husbandry UNESP/Botucatu, from August 1989 to July 1990. The flock has approximately 200 sheep which are raised in paddocks on a rotational system. The forage is mainly coast-cross grass (Cynodon dactylon) and the mean stocking rate is 20 animals per hectare. Parturitions occur in July, August and September; tail docking is done one or two months after birth, and shearing in December.

The animals were checked daily, and the body region affected, the age of the animal and observation date were recorded.

At two-week intervals larvae from wounds were collected and sent to the Laboratory of Parasitology of the Department of Parasitology, Institute of Biological Science UNESP/Botucatu. These larvae were transferred to petri dishes containing a culture medium composed of ground beef and citrated blood. The petri dishes were maintained at 35~ and the culture medium was changed twice daily until the larvae left it spontaneously for pupation. The pupae were maintained at 27"C in vials containing moist sawdust. Emerging flies were identified according to Guimar~es (1983). Climatic data were obtained from the Department of Environmental Sciences of the College of Agriculture UNESP/Botucatu. The correlation coefficient of the myiasis number with the average temperature, rainfall and relative humidity was calculated.

### RESULTS
Larvae of D. hominis and C. hominivorax only were found causing myiases during the observation period. Myiasis due to C. homhTivorax was observed throughout the year with a high incidence from January to April. The highest infestation occurred in the warmest months coinciding with high rainfall and humidity (Fig. 1). The correlation of myiasis number with average temperature was high (r = 0-74; P < 0-01), while it was low with rainfall (r = 0"25; P > 0'05). Yet there was a significant correlation between myiasis number and relative humidity (r = 0-57; P < 0.05).

The body regions most frequently infested with C, hominivorax larvae were the feet, vulva, tail and scrotum (Table I). Infestation of the feet was observed from December to July coinciding with a foot-rot outbreak in the flock. Tail myiasis was only observed in animals that had been docked.

In almost every month C. hominivorax larvae were observed parasitising the vulva, however, the highest prevalence occurred during the warmest months and was coincident with oestrus. After shearing, many cases of dermatobiosis were observed particularly in the scrotum of rams.
