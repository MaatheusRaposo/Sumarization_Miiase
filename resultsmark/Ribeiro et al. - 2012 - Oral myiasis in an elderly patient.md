Background: Myiasis is the invasion of living tissue of humans and other mammals by eggs or maggots of flies of the order of Diptera. It occurs mainly in the Tropics and is associated with inadequate public and personal hygiene. Oral myiases in an older man appears to be rare. Objective: To relate a case of oral myiases in a debilitated older man treated by mechanical removal of the maggots, identifying the adult insect that caused the infestation. Methods: The diagnosis of oral myiasis was established by the clinical examination and it was detected that the infestation involved only soft tissue and the sinus cavity. The patient was submitted to two mechanical removal of the visible maggots. Results: Total of 110 maggots was removed from the oral cavity of the patient and adult insects was identified as belonging to the Calliphoridae Family, Cochliomyia hominivorax species. The patient died two days after the second procedure by severe systemic complications. Conclusions: The mechanic removal and the identification of the maggots must be adopted as soon as possible to prevent further tissue damage and bacterial infection in cases of oral myiasis. Special attention should be given to the debilitated old patients that are particularly susceptible to oral myiasis infestation.

### Introduction
Myiasis is the infestation of humans and vertebrate animals with dipterous larvae, which feed on the host's dead or living tissues, liquid body substances, or ingested food. Maggots can infest any organ or tissue accessible to fly oviposition, most cases probably occur with a female fly depositing either eggs or larvae on necrotic tissue, open wounds, and unbroken skin or mucosa of animal or human host 1 . Such invasions may be benign in effect, asymptomatic, may result in mild to violent disturbances, or even death 2,3 . Eighty different species of Diptera have been known to invade man 4 .

Oral myiasis was first described by Laurence in 1909 5 (two cases) and occurs in humans mainly in the Tropics. It is associated with poor oral hygiene, alcoholism, senility, severe halitosis, suppurating lesions and others conditions. A case of myiasis caused by Cochliomyia hominivorax in a debilitated man is presented with extensive infestation of maggots in the jaws that were treated by surgical removal.

### Case Report
A 97-year-old white male from Cravinhos, State of Sao Paulo, Brazil, was admitted to Sao Francisco Hospital. His relatives described the presence of maggots inside his mouth. An immediately evaluation by the Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery team was requested. The patient had a positive medical history for intestinal, dermatological (skin cancer) and heart diseases and had been bedridden for a number of years. During physical examination he was found to be cachectic, conscious but not responsive, mouth breathing with the oral cavity permanently open, and being fed by a nasogastric catheter for the last 8 months.

Oral examination revealed the remaining teeth in both jaws to be in a very poor condition, with ulcerated and necrosed mucosal tissue, a bad odour and the presence of maggots. The diagnosis of oral myiasis was established and X-rays were taken of the frontal and lateral skull (Fig. 1). Fortunately, the infestation involved only soft tissue and the sinus cavity. The patient was first submitted to the removal of the visible maggots. A total of 20 maggots were removed, which showed a spiral form and varied in size from 5 to 10 mm (Fig. 2). Due to the poor condition of the patient, a second procedure was planned, this being for removal of remaining maggots and extraction of teeth that were in poor condition, with debridement of the necrotic tissue.

During the second evaluation, a re-infested wound was revealed with several maggots two or three times larger than previously described A surgical procedure was performed under local anaesthesia (2% xylocaine with 1:100 000 epinephrine). Fifteen teeth were extracted and all visible maggots, totalling 80, were removed. The   wound was irrigated with saline, debrided and sutured with Vicryl 3-0 in the maxilla and mandible (Fig. 3). Post-operative care and recommendations were given to the family of the patient. One day after the surgery, the patient presented requiring special care and was taken to the Intensive Care Unit. A new evaluation was carried out and the presence of 10 more maggots in the oral cavity, dehiscence of the previous sutures and the presence of necrotic tissues was recorded. The patient was intubated but was severely ill, and unfortunately died 2 days later.

In all the surgical interventions, a total of 110 maggots had been removed from the oral cavity of the patient. The maggots were sent to the Laboratory of Parasitology of the School of Pharmaceutical Sciences of the University of Sao Paulo for identification. The maggots at the third stage were 15 mm in length, white-orange in colour, with pigmented tracheal trunks until the third segment. After 2 days, they transformed into brown barrel-shaped puparia. The puparium stage terminated 9 days later with the emergence of adults.

The adult flies presented on average from 8-10 mm in length, with a greenish-blue metallic colour, a yellow face, reddish eyes and three dark stripes on the dorsal surface of the thorax. In accordance with the morphological characteristics observed and using taxonomic keys, the larvae and adult insects had been identified as belonging to the Calliphoridae Family, Cochliomyia hominivorax species.

### Discussion
Oral myiasis occurs following direct infestation of oral tissue with the eggs of the flies [1][2][3][4][5] . The patient in the present case was a mouth breather, with his mouth permanently open, bedridden, and senile with ulcerative necrotic lesions in the oral cavity. The precarious oral hygiene, intense halitosis, mouth breathing and senility could have facilitated the infestation. Once the maggots were present, an aggravation of the patient's general health state was triggered, causing a severe acute inflamatory reaction of tissues, resulting in pain and extreme discomfort.

Cases alrealdy seen in the literature describe patients who may have poor oral hygiene 6,7 , a weak nutritional state, senility, trauma, paralysis, unconsciousness, mental debility, alcoholic, fingersucking habits, mouth breathers or people who maintain their mouths open for long periods of time, outdoors or residence in agricultural zones 5,8,9 .

Other cases of oral myiasis have been reported in epileptic patients with lacerated lips following seizure, in children with incompetent lips and thumb-sucking habits, in patients with advanced periodontal disease, tooth extration sites, in a fungating carcinoma of the buccal mucosa and in a patient with tetanus who had his mouth propped open to maintain the airway [9][10][11] .

In Goia ´s State, Brazil, myiasis was commonly found in poor and malnourished people who lived alone or were cared for by relatives who had little concern for their well-being. Patients with neurological, psychiatric pathologies, physical deficiencies and bedridden individuals presented with lesions with a higher numbers of maggots in the periodontal tissues and the tongue 9,14 .

A study by Fernandes et al. 10 indicated that C. hominivorax is the predominant agent of human myiases in the New World. In Brazil, C. hominivorax maggots were identified in cases of oral myiases 9,12 . The larvae are an obligatory parasite that generally use mucous membranes or wounds as entry points and grow on the healthy tissue of their live host and destroy the tissues.

The adults females may lay more than 2800 eggs, deposited in batches of 10 to 400 eggs each and the incubation period ranges from 11 to 21 h. The larval feeding period ranges from 3.5 to 4.5 days or more [2][3][4] and the number of maggots that develop in a specific lesion is directly proportional to the tissue damage. Severe infections may lead to death of the host, which enphasises the importance of an early diagnosis.

Removal of the maggots is the most prudent treatment and must be adopted as soon as possible to prevent further tissue damage and bacterial infection [11][12][13][14] .

Prevention of oral myiasis involves good hygiene but old, bedridden and debilitated patients are particularly at risk and special attention should be given to dental and oral hygiene 9,10,13,15 .
